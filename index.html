Choose Your Own Adventure

DECEMBER 2024

The Christmas Business Chaos

It is early December, and you realise you need extra money for Christmas — presents, snacks, and possibly a new hoodie because the school heating has definitely broken again. So you decide to start a business for the Christmas period.

But your decisions will determine whether you end up as a Christmas Business Legend… or bankrupt and forced to work as an Elf in a garden centre. Choose wisely.

Start Hot Chocolate Pop-Up Path A Start Gift Hamper Business Path B Start Festive Dog-Walking Path C

⭐ PATH A – HOT CHOCOLATE POP-UP

GCSE Business meets Christmas chaos.

🎄 A1 – Opening Day at Your Hot Chocolate Stall

You set up your stall outside school during the Christmas fair.

There is snow ☃️, music 🎶, and a suspicious smell of burnt mince pies. Your stall looks amazing because: your USP is “The Ultimate Hot Chocolate Experience”, you offer flavoured syrups, whipped cream towers, and optional sprinkles shaped like accountants, and you’ve positioned yourself at a high footfall location.

Demand instantly spikes. You have created… a queue. A long queue. But now you face your first real business decision.

⭐ A1 – Supplier Choice

  • ❄️ CHOICE A1a – Buy from the Cheap Wholesaler
    • Very low variable costs, Higher profit margin, Possible quality issues 👀, Delivery speed: fast (very fast — suspiciously fast)
  • 🎁 CHOICE A1b – Buy from a Premium Local Café Supplier
    • Higher variable cost, Excellent quality → stronger brand image, Helps differentiate your product in a competitive market, Delivery speed: slow (they make everything by hand while telling you about their feelings)
A1a: Cheap Wholesaler A1b: Premium Local Café

⭐⭐ PATH A1a – THE CHEAP WHOLESALER

The wholesaler delivers a huge bag of marshmallows labelled: “Marshmallow-Type Product (Suitable For Some Uses)”. **Red flag. 🚩**

A Year 8 yells: **“WHY DOES MY MARSHMALLOW TASTE LIKE… COAT?”**

Your products suffer a quality failure, leading to: ❌ Drop in customer satisfaction, ❌ Negative word-of-mouth promotion, ❌ A 30% fall in demand, ❌ Threat of brand damage. You must respond quickly.

⭐ A1a(i) – Crisis Response

  • 🍫 CHOICE A1a(i)-1 – Launch a Promotional Discount

    You drop prices by 20% (a sales promotion strategy) hoping to regain market share and encourage repeat purchases.

  • 🌱 CHOICE A1a(i)-2 – Rebrand as “Eco-Friendly Vegan Marshmallows”

    You plan to reposition your brand to target the ethical consumer segment.

Discount Rebrand

⭐⭐ PATH A1b – PREMIUM SUPPLIER

The premium marshmallows arrive in beautiful compostable packaging. Customers rave about your taste, your presentation, your vibe, your festive playlist (mostly Michael Bublé). Your reputation skyrockets.

BUT… Your costs are rising too. Your cash flow forecast shows that if you continue like this, you’ll break even on 23 December at 14:07, which is useless because school closes on the 20th. So it’s time for a pricing strategy.

⭐ A1b(i) – PRICING

  • 💷 CHOICE A1b(i)-1 – Price Skimming

    You raise your price because your product is high quality and demand is inelastic (people are COLD).

  • 🎁 CHOICE A1b(i)-2 – Bundle Deals

    “Buy 2, get a candy cane FREE!” (A classic value-added promotion to encourage sales volume.)

Price Skimming Bundle Deals

🎁 PATH B – THE CHRISTMAS GIFT HAMPER BUSINESS

A beautiful blend of enterprise skills, market segmentation… and absolute chaos.

⭐ B1 – Launching Your Hampers

You decide to start a Christmas Gift Hamper business. Your hampers include: hot chocolate sachets, candles that smell like “Winter Cinnamon Corporate Strategy,” questionable tinsel, and a handwritten note saying “Merry Businessmas 🎄📈”.

You have a huge potential market — but who should you target first?

⭐⭐ CHOICE B1 – Target Market

  • CHOICE B1a – Target Students
    • ✔ Big customer base, ✔ Love sweets, ✔ Constantly forget they have to pay you
  • CHOICE B1b – Target Teachers
    • ✔ Higher income, ✔ Emotional need for festive stress relief, ✔ Scarily high expectations
Target Students Target Teachers

⭐⭐⭐ PATH B1a – Targeting Students

Students LOVE your hamper idea, but have unusual preferences (Pokémon cards, ONLY crisps). Customer needs vary. Market segmentation becomes a NIGHTMARE.

⭐ B1a(i) – Order Management System

  • 📋 CHOICE B1a(i)-1 – Use a Google Form

    ✔ Structured, ✔ Easy data collection, ✔ Helps conduct market research, ✔ Reduces communication errors.

  • 📱 CHOICE B1a(i)-2 – Use Instagram DMs

    ✔ Easy, ✔ Students already use Insta, ✖ Absolutely no record-keeping, ✖ A guaranteed administrative apocalypse.

Google Form Instagram DMs

⭐⭐⭐ B1a(i)-1 – Google Form Success

Your form helps you gather: customer budgets, product preferences, allergen information, and “gift message” requests. Production becomes smooth. Quality control improves. Stakeholders are happy.

Demand is high, but your costs are rising due to inflation in “festive glitter” prices. You must set your price.

⭐ B1a(i)-1(a) – Next Decision: Pricing Strategy

  • 💷 CHOICE B1a(i)-1(a)-1 – Cost-Plus Pricing

    Add a 40% markup to guarantee profit.

  • 🎁 CHOICE B1a(i)-1(a)-2 – Psychological Pricing (£4.99 instead of £5.00)

    Because students fall for it every time.

Cost-Plus Psychological

⭐⭐⭐ B1a(i)-2 – Instagram DM Chaos

Your DMs are full of: half messages, typos, people ordering hampers at 3:47am, and someone asking “do you accept Tesco Clubcard points?”.

You lose 14 orders immediately. Your cash flow becomes impossible to track. You accidentally send the wrong hamper to a parent, and their confused nan receives one with a cat toy inside.

⭐ B1a(i)-2(a) – Crisis Response

  • 🔍 CHOICE B1a(i)-2(a)-1 – Hire a friend to manage admin (£5/hour)

    This is called recruitment. And exploitation.

  • 🧹 CHOICE B1a(i)-2(a)-2 – Close Instagram orders and switch to paper forms

    Low-tech but reliable.

Hire Friend Paper Forms

⭐⭐⭐ PATH B1b – Targeting Teachers

Teachers LOVE the idea of custom gift hampers. Your packaging is elegant. Your pricing is premium. Your branding is classy (“Festive Flair Hampers™”). After your first day, you receive glowing reviews like: “This is better than John Lewis.”

BUT… Your supplier suddenly runs out of the luxury Belgian chocolate that teachers specifically want. **Your supply chain has collapsed.**

⭐ B1b(i) – Supply Chain Decision

  • 🛒 CHOICE B1b(i)-1 – Switch to a Cheaper Supplier

    ✔ Lower purchase price, ✔ Higher profit margin, ✖ Teachers WILL notice, ✖ Risk to brand image.

  • 💎 CHOICE B1b(i)-2 – Increase Price Due to Scarcity

    ✔ Premium branding, ✔ Scarcity marketing, ✔ Possibly boosts perceived value, ✖ Risk of reduced demand.

Cheaper Supplier Scarcity Pricing

🐕‍🦺 PATH C – THE FESTIVE DOG-WALKING SERVICE

An entrepreneurial journey filled with ethics, customer service… and dogs in Santa hats.

⭐ C1 – Launch Day

You start your festive dog-walking business. Every dog receives: a tiny Santa hat, a mini Christmas bandana, and a personalised treat bag labelled “BARKeting Department”.

But one dog, a tiny but chaotic pug named Sir Wigglebottom McSniffington III, **escapes during a walk.** This is a major operational failure.

⭐⭐ CHOICE C1 – Crisis Response

  • CHOICE C1a – Launch a Search Operation
    • ✔ Stakeholder satisfaction, ✔ Ethical behaviour, ✔ Higher costs (transport, time)
  • CHOICE C1b – Pretend Nothing Happened
    • ✔ Saves time, ✔ Zero cost, ✖ INSANELY high risk, ✖ Terrible customer service
Search Operation Ignore It

⭐⭐⭐ PATH C1a – The Search Operation

You print posters, ask neighbours, and run an emergency promotional campaign titled: **“FIND THE PUG – FREE TREATS FOR ANY INFORMATION”** (Customer engagement = excellent).

You find Sir Wigglebottom asleep inside a neighbour’s nativity display, curled up next to Baby Jesus. You now must decide your public relations strategy.

⭐ C1a(i) – PR Response

  • 📢 CHOICE C1a(i)-1 – Turn It Into a Hero Story

    Post photos, create content, build brand loyalty, celebrate responsible behaviour.

  • 📝 CHOICE C1a(i)-2 – Issue a Formal Apology

    Shows professionalism. May reduce customer complaints.

Hero Story Formal Apology

⭐⭐⭐ PATH C1b – The “Ignore It” Strategy

You decide to avoid responsibility. Bold choice. Terrible choice. Sir Wigglebottom DOES return home… wearing a random Santa hat and carrying a stolen sausage.

The owner blames YOU. Your customer satisfaction plummets.

⭐ C1b(i) – Damage Control

  • 💸 CHOICE C1b(i)-1 – Offer a Refund

    ✔ Reduces complaints, ✖ Destroys your profit margin, ✖ Encourages more complaints (“free money???”).

  • 🎄 CHOICE C1b(i)-2 – Offer a Loyalty Card

    “Every 5 walks = 1 free.” ✔ Encourages repeat business, ✔ Builds loyalty, ✖ Reduces short-term profit.

Refund Ruin Loyalty Card

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1a(i)-1 – THE LOSS LEADER LEGEND

Your discount works — demand increases again. Although your profit margin shrinks dramatically, your revenue increases. You become locally famous: Someone even comments, **“This is better value than Starbucks.”** You don’t make much profit… But you do learn the meaning of loss leaders and customer loyalty.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** penetration pricing, loss leaders, demand elasticity, customer loyalty, revenue vs profit.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1a(i)-2 – THE SUSTAINABILITY SCANDAL

You panic and reply: **“They are… spiritually vegan.”** This triggers: A complaint from a parent, An email from the PTA, A safeguarding concern (???), Someone makes a TikTok calling you **“Marshmallow Walter White”**.

Your business collapses due to: ethical failure, stakeholder pressure, and a breakdown in brand trust.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** ethics, CSR, stakeholder conflict, reputation, legal implications, branding.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1b(i)-1 – THE PREMIUM POWER MOVE

You raise your price by £1. Nobody complains. You achieve: ✔ Higher gross profit, ✔ Stronger premium brand positioning, ✔ Break-even earlier than expected, **✔ Total profit: £312**. You feel like a young Alan Sugar.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** skimming, price elasticity of demand, break-even, profit margins.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1b(i)-2 – THE BUNDLE OVERLOAD

Everyone buys the bundle… but only eats the FREE candy cane. Your variable costs rise. Your profit margin collapses.

You end the day with: 61 untouched hot chocolates, 0 candy canes, and sadness. You reflect deeply on pricing strategy failure.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** sales promotions, margin impact, revenue vs profit, misforecasting.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING B1a(i)-1(a)-1 – THE CALCULATED SUCCESS

Your cost-plus pricing ensures consistent gross profit. You hit your break-even point by Day 3. You reinvest profits into buying luxury chocolate and instantly improve your USP. Students call you **“the Jeff Bezos of Christmas.”**

**GCSE Concepts Used:** cost-plus pricing, break-even, reinvestment, USP, cash flow.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING B1a(i)-1(a)-2 – THE 4.99 TRIUMPH

Students LOVE the perceived bargain. Sales double. Your contribution per unit rises. **You make £176 profit in three days.** A Year 10 says: “£4.99 feels so cheap, I’ll buy two.” You whisper: “Perfect.”

**GCSE Concepts Used:** psychological pricing, demand, consumer behaviour, profit maximisation.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING B1a(i)-2(a)-1 – THE BAD HIRE

Your friend is extremely enthusiastic but deeply incompetent. They double-book orders, lose money, and accidentally create a hamper labelled: “To my favourite goat.”

You learn the meaning of poor recruitment, low labour productivity, and catastrophic wage expenses. Business collapses.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** recruitment, productivity, wage expenses, administrative failure.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING B1a(i)-2(a)-2 – THE PAPER FORM COMEBACK

Paper forms reduce mistakes. Organisation improves. Demand stabilises. **You make £95 profit,** which you immediately spend on snacks. A perfectly acceptable outcome.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** low-tech solutions, efficiency, profit stability.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ Ending B1b(i)-1(a) – The Quality Crisis

You switch to low-cost chocolate. The complaints begin: **“I detected a lack of cocoa depth.”** Your quality assurance has failed. Your brand reputation plummets. You attempt a refund programme. It destroys your cash flow. **You end the week with –£43 profit** and a box of leftover sad chocolate.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** quality assurance failure, brand damage, cash flow destruction, supply chain issues.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ Ending B1b(i)-2(a) – The Luxury Scarcity Strategy

You raise prices by 20% and rebrand your hampers as: ✨ “LIMITED EDITION LUXURY BELGIAN HAMPERS (Only 6 Available)” ✨. Teachers love this. Scarcity triggers demand. Your hampers sell out instantly. **You finish the week with £180 profit.** You whisper: **“Adam Smith would be proud.”**

**GCSE Concepts Used:** scarcity marketing, premium pricing, revenue increase, profit maximisation.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING C1a(i)-1 – THE SOCIAL MEDIA HERO

Your post goes viral. Caption: **“We found Sir Wigglebottom living his best life in the nativity scene 🐕✨🎄”**. Dog owners LOVE you. Demand doubles. Other businesses invite you to collaborate. **You make £210 profit** and receive three dog Christmas cards.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** positive PR, social media promotion, brand loyalty, collaboration.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING C1a(i)-2 – THE PROFESSIONAL MANAGER

Your apology is so well written that someone asks if you're studying English Lit instead of Business. Customers trust you more. Your reputation becomes **“responsible and reliable,”** a key factor in brand loyalty. **You earn £125 profit** and experience minimal stress.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** professionalism, reputation building, customer trust, brand loyalty.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING C1b(i)-1 – THE REFUND RUIN

You refund the owner. Then two more people claim their dogs were “emotionally distressed.” You refund them too. **Your profit collapses to £0.** Sir Wigglebottom now barks every time he sees you.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** refund policy, financial loss, negative customer behaviour, brand deterioration.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING C1b(i)-2 – THE ACCIDENTAL SUCCESS

Owners LOVE loyalty schemes. Suddenly everyone wants you to walk their dogs. Your customer base grows. **Your profit rises to £95.** You celebrate by buying more Santa hats for dogs. A happy ending.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** loyalty schemes, repeat business, customer base growth.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos
