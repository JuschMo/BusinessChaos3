⭐ CHOOSE YOUR OWN ADVENTURE: “THE CHRISTMAS BUSINESS CHAOS”

A GCSE Business Story with Festive Mayhem

🎄 INTRO

It is early December, and you realise you need extra money for Christmas — presents, snacks, and possibly a new hoodie because the school heating has definitely broken again. So you decide to start a business for the Christmas period.

But your decisions will determine whether you end up as a Christmas Business Legend… or bankrupt and forced to work as an Elf in a garden centre. **Choose wisely.**

⭐ CHOICE 1 – WHAT BUSINESS DO YOU OPEN?

  • A: A Hot Chocolate Pop-up Stall USP: extra toppings, flavoured syrups, marshmallow mountains. Stakeholders: customers (cold), school staff (grumpy), supplier (local café)
  • B: A Christmas Gift Hamper Business USP: personalised gift bundles. Stakeholders: parents, students, suppliers, the PTA (who will definitely want a discount)
  • C: A Festive Dog-Walking Service USP: dogs get a free Santa hat. Stakeholders: pet owners, neighbours, possibly the RSPCA
Choose A: Hot Chocolate Pop-up Choose B: Gift Hamper Business Choose C: Dog-Walking Service

⭐ PATH A – HOT CHOCOLATE POP-UP

GCSE Business meets Christmas chaos.

🎄 A1 – Opening Day at Your Hot Chocolate Stall

You set up your stall outside school during the Christmas fair.

There is snow ☃️, music 🎶, and a suspicious smell of burnt mince pies. Your stall looks amazing because:

  • ✔ your USP is “The Ultimate Hot Chocolate Experience”
  • ✔ you offer flavoured syrups, whipped cream towers, and optional sprinkles shaped like accountants
  • ✔ you’ve positioned yourself at a high footfall location

Demand instantly spikes. You have created… a queue. A long queue. But now you face your first real business decision.

⭐ A1 – Supplier Choice

Your first batch nearly sells out. You urgently need more stock.

  • ❄️ CHOICE A1a – Buy from the Cheap Wholesaler
    • Very low variable costs
    • Higher profit margin
    • Possible quality issues 👀
    • Delivery speed: fast (very fast — suspiciously fast)
  • 🎁 CHOICE A1b – Buy from a Premium Local Café Supplier
    • Higher variable cost
    • Excellent quality → stronger brand image
    • Helps differentiate your product in a competitive market
    • Delivery speed: slow (they make everything by hand while telling you about their feelings)
Choose A1a: Cheap Wholesaler Choose A1b: Premium Local Café

⭐⭐ PATH A1a – THE CHEAP WHOLESALER

The wholesaler delivers a huge bag of marshmallows labelled: “Marshmallow-Type Product (Suitable For Some Uses)”. **Red flag. 🚩**

But you choose to ignore it. At first, customers don’t notice — your presentation is so good that people take more photos than sips.

But then… A Year 8 yells: **“WHY DOES MY MARSHMALLOW TASTE LIKE… COAT?”**

Word spreads on Snapchat at the speed of light. Your products suffer a quality failure, leading to:

  • ❌ Drop in customer satisfaction
  • ❌ Negative word-of-mouth promotion
  • ❌ A 30% fall in demand
  • ❌ Threat of brand damage

You must respond quickly.

⭐ A1a(i) – Crisis Response

  • 🍫 CHOICE A1a(i)-1 – Launch a Promotional Discount

    You drop prices by 20% (a sales promotion strategy) hoping to regain market share and encourage repeat purchases.

  • 🌱 CHOICE A1a(i)-2 – Rebrand as “Eco-Friendly Vegan Marshmallows”

    You plan to reposition your brand to target the ethical consumer segment.

Choose A1a(i)-1: Discount Choose A1a(i)-2: Rebrand

⭐⭐ PATH A1b – PREMIUM SUPPLIER

The premium marshmallows arrive in beautiful compostable packaging. Your stall now radiates artisan Christmas street-market energy.

Customers rave about your taste, your presentation, your vibe, your festive playlist (mostly Michael Bublé). Your reputation skyrockets.

BUT… Your costs are rising too. Your cash flow forecast shows that if you continue like this, you’ll break even on 23 December at 14:07, which is useless because school closes on the 20th. So it’s time for a pricing strategy.

⭐ A1b(i) – PRICING

  • 💷 CHOICE A1b(i)-1 – Price Skimming

    You raise your price because your product is high quality and demand is inelastic (people are COLD).

  • 🎁 CHOICE A1b(i)-2 – Bundle Deals

    “Buy 2, get a candy cane FREE!” (A classic value-added promotion to encourage sales volume.)

Choose A1b(i)-1: Price Skimming Choose A1b(i)-2: Bundle Deals

🎁 PATH B – THE CHRISTMAS GIFT HAMPER BUSINESS

A beautiful blend of enterprise skills, market segmentation… and absolute chaos.

⭐ B1 – Launching Your Hampers

You decide to start a Christmas Gift Hamper business, because nothing says “entrepreneurial spirit” like buying cheap chocolate in bulk and repackaging it expensively.

Your hampers include: hot chocolate sachets, candles that smell like “Winter Cinnamon Corporate Strategy,” questionable tinsel, and a handwritten note saying “Merry Businessmas 🎄📈”.

You have a huge potential market — but who should you target first?

⭐⭐ CHOICE B1 – Target Market

  • CHOICE B1a – Target Students
    • ✔ Big customer base
    • ✔ Love sweets
    • ✔ Constantly forget they have to pay you
  • CHOICE B1b – Target Teachers
    • ✔ Higher income
    • ✔ Emotional need for festive stress relief
    • ✔ Scarily high expectations
Choose B1a: Target Students Choose B1b: Target Teachers

🐕‍🦺 PATH C – THE FESTIVE DOG-WALKING SERVICE

An entrepreneurial journey filled with ethics, customer service… and dogs in Santa hats.

⭐ C1 – Launch Day

You start your festive dog-walking business. Every dog receives: a tiny Santa hat, a mini Christmas bandana, and a personalised treat bag labelled “BARKeting Department”.

Demand grows quickly — because people want to enjoy Christmas shopping without their pets eating wrapping paper.

But one dog, a tiny but chaotic pug named Sir Wigglebottom McSniffington III, **escapes during a walk.** This is a major operational failure.

⭐⭐ CHOICE C1 – Crisis Response

  • CHOICE C1a – Launch a Search Operation
    • ✔ Stakeholder satisfaction
    • ✔ Ethical behaviour
    • ✔ Higher costs (transport, time)
  • CHOICE C1b – Pretend Nothing Happened
    • ✔ Saves time
    • ✔ Zero cost
    • ✖ INSANELY high risk
    • ✖ Terrible customer service
Choose C1a: Search Operation Choose C1b: Ignore It

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1a(i)-1 – THE LOSS LEADER LEGEND

Your discount works — demand increases again. Although your profit margin shrinks dramatically, your revenue increases.

You become locally famous: Someone even comments, **“This is better value than Starbucks.”**

You don’t make much profit… But you do learn the meaning of loss leaders and customer loyalty.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** penetration pricing, loss leaders, demand elasticity, customer loyalty, revenue vs profit.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1a(i)-2 – THE SUSTAINABILITY SCANDAL

A Year 10 asks: **“Are the marshmallows actually vegan?”** You panic and reply: **“They are… spiritually vegan.”**

This triggers: A complaint from a parent, An email from the PTA, A safeguarding concern (???), Someone makes a TikTok calling you **“Marshmallow Walter White”**.

Your business collapses due to: ethical failure, stakeholder pressure, and a breakdown in brand trust.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** ethics, CSR, stakeholder conflict, reputation, legal implications, branding.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1b(i)-1 – THE PREMIUM POWER MOVE

You raise your price by £1. Nobody complains. In fact, people think it tastes BETTER because it’s more expensive.

You achieve: ✔ Higher gross profit, ✔ Stronger premium brand positioning, ✔ Break-even earlier than expected, **✔ Total profit: £312**.

You feel like a young Alan Sugar.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** skimming, price elasticity of demand, break-even, profit margins.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ ENDING A1b(i)-2 – THE BUNDLE OVERLOAD

Everyone buys the bundle… but only eats the FREE candy cane. Your variable costs rise. Your profit margin collapses.

You end the day with: 61 untouched hot chocolates, 0 candy canes, and sadness.

You reflect deeply on pricing strategy failure.

**GCSE Concepts Used:** sales promotions, margin impact, revenue vs profit, misforecasting.
Restart The Christmas Business Chaos

⭐⭐⭐ PATH B1a – Targeting Students

Students LOVE your hamper idea. Within minutes, you receive 37 requests. Within hours, you realise students have… unusual preferences.

Examples include: “Can you put ONLY crisps in mine?”, “Can there be a photo of my dog dressed as a reindeer?”, “I want a hamper but I can only pay you in Pokémon cards.”

Customer needs vary. Market segmentation becomes a NIGHTMARE.

⭐ B1a(i) – Order Management System

  • 📋 CHOICE B1a(i)-1 – Use a Google Form

    ✔ Structured, ✔ Easy data collection, ✔ Helps conduct market research, ✔ Reduces communication errors.

  • 📱 CHOICE B1a(i)-2 – Use Instagram DMs

    ✔ Easy, ✔ Students already use Insta, ✖ Absolutely no record-keeping, ✖ A guaranteed administrative apocalypse.

Choose B1a(i)-1: Google Form Choose B1a(i)-2: Instagram DMs
